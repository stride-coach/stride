# Product information architecture

- Status: Proposed for discovery
- Inputs: [Product brief](../product/brief.md), [draft PRD](../product/prd.md), and [design system direction](design-system.md)

## URLs and page titles

Route segments, query parameter names and query parameter values are English, even though every visible label is French. Renamed French URLs redirect permanently to their English equivalent in `next.config.ts`. Every page sets a short French title; the root layout template renders it as `<Page> · Stride`, with the section after the page when it helps (`Records · Progression · Stride`), and `Stride` alone when a page has none.

## Navigation recommendation

Use a small set of athlete jobs as the primary navigation. Do not expose every data domain or AI specialist as a top-level destination.

| Destination | Athlete question                             | Candidate contents                                                       |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Today       | What matters for my next training decision?  | Next session, concise context, readiness caveats, and a route into Coach |
| Plan        | What am I training toward, and what is next? | Weekly plan, goal event, schedule, and reviewed plan changes             |
| Activities  | What have I done?                            | Road and trail history, activity details, and sync freshness             |
| Santé       | How is my body and my form changing?         | Fitness, recovery, health, nutrition and records from tested measures    |
| Courses     | Am I ready for my goal races?                | Season, race strategy, course, nutrition, gear and preparation           |
| Coach       | What should I understand or discuss?         | Context-aware conversation and explainable proposals                     |

The desktop shell follows the Stride Design System pages « La navigation » and « La mise en page et la hauteur ». A 48px top bar on the `shell` background holds the sidebar toggle, the logo, the season switcher, « Demander au coach » (opening the main thread at `/coach`), the notification bell and the account avatar. The bell shows a `primary` dot when something is unread, never a count. The avatar opens the account menu: Modèle d’athlète, Réglages (⌘,), Connexions, Matériel, Données et confidentialité, the theme (Système, Clair, Sombre), keyboard shortcuts and sign out. The 212px sidebar lists six spaces, Aujourd’hui, Plan, Activités, Santé, Courses and Coach, with the data status at its bottom, and folds to a 52px icon rail with ⌘B; Plan and Santé show their sections below the spaces while open. Contour lines of the relief sit in the frame behind the sidebar and the top bar and show faintly through the content panel, never behind cards, banners or fields. The content is a 12px-radius panel at 70% of `background` whose page header is 44px high; cards, banners and fields inside it stay opaque. With an active plan, the season switcher names the season shown on Plan (the current one on every other screen) with its week, « S2 / 25 », and opens a menu of the plan's seasons under « Saison en cours », « À venir » and « Passées »: priority mark, name, dates and race day, week x/y, week count, « Terminée » or « Remplacé le <jour mois> », the countdown, a check on the selected season, and « Toutes les courses ». « Passées » lists the past seasons of the active plan and the archived plans together, most recent first. Choosing a season opens Plan on it; nothing else follows the choice, and it is not stored. Without an active plan, the top bar keeps the goal race with its countdown, linking to Courses, except on Plan when archived plans exist, where « Saisons » opens the same menu. The switcher replaces the programme selector of #415. Below 768px each page header is the 52px mobile top bar: the page title and detail, the goal race countdown linking to Courses, the compact data status, the bell and the account avatar with the same account menu. A 62px bottom bar holds five equal tabs, Aujourd’hui, Plan, Coach, Activités and Santé, with the waiting count on Coach, and stays visible in the main thread and in topics, under the composer. There is no navigation sheet: settings, gear and the theme are in the account menu and Courses is behind the countdown. Matériel is an Activités tab at `/activities/gear` and Nutrition a Santé tab at `/health/nutrition`; the former `/gear/*` and `/nutrition` URLs redirect permanently there.

A destination appears in the application navigation only once it is backed by real data; there are no placeholder entries. Activities and Courses are available to onboarded athletes. Activity list and detail show only stored Garmin values with their provenance, plus the plan status Stride derives from session matches. The list groups activities by Monday-based week; each week header shows the activity count, km, D+ and time over the whole week for the current sport filter and, when the active plan has sessions that week, « Plan : n séances sur m · k hors plan ». The list header holds the « Sorties » and « Matériel » tabs, the name search and the sport filter (Tout, Course, Trail, Renfo, Vélo, Autres), which hides empty sports except Tout and the selected one. From 1024 px the list is a table (date, activity, planned intensity, duration, distance, D+, pace, HR, compliance); on mobile each row keeps the title, a summary line, the duration and the compliance. A matched row shows the planned intensity chip and its duration compliance as « n % », or the short verdict « <famille> · <verdict court> » when Garmin gives no duration; unmatched rows show « Hors plan » when a plan exists, and recent unplanned activities without a feeling (current or previous week) show « Ressenti à noter ». An activity opens on its own page, with a breadcrumb back to the list that keeps the filter, search and pagination; from 1280 px the shoe check, time in zones and effort sit in a context panel beside it. Natural-language search and the race filter remain unavailable. Sync times and the athlete's current day use the Europe/Paris time zone.

On desktop the application shell is fixed to the viewport height and the page itself never scrolls. Each pane scrolls on its own: the Activities list keeps its title, search and filters fixed with sticky period headers, and the detail keeps its header fixed while its content scrolls. The list shows 50 activities at a time with a count and a load-more action, and remembers its scroll position when an activity is opened. Selection, sport filter, literal imported-name search and the loaded window live in the URL and survive navigation and return. Name search runs before counting and pagination, ignores case and treats wildcards and punctuation literally; submitting a new search resets the loaded window. Empty results retain the search and type controls.

The activity page holds everything; `/activities/[id]/details` redirects to `/activities/[id]` with the same query. In order: the kicker (date, time, place), up to six headline measures (Distance, Temps, Allure, D+, FC moy., Puissance) and, for runs, the shoe line with « Changer »; then the plan card. A matched activity shows « Séance du plan » with its pill (from the full debrief verdict), the verdict sentence, « Voir dans le plan », the feeling and « En parler au coach » with the session attached. An unplanned activity shows « Hors plan » with how it counts in the week's running load against the planned km (only with an active plan), the feeling prompt and « En parler au coach », which opens `/coach/new?activity=<id>` with the activity attached; an unplanned activity cannot be attached to a session by hand. For runs, a one-line comparison with the last three similar earlier runs (same session family when planned, same sport within ±25 % of the distance otherwise) opens a table behind « Détail ▾ »; « à FC égale » is a Stride estimate labelled as such, and the line is hidden without an earlier similar run. « Parcours et données » shows the route map (or the « Parcours » empty state without GPS) above one chart whose series (Allure, FC, Puissance, Cadence, Altitude) are toggled freely with at least one shown, Allure and FC by default; the map point follows the chart cursor. Then the laps table (D− and power included; on narrow screens Tour, Temps, Allure, FC, D+/D−, W), time in zones next to the effort list, and the Garmin import provenance. Missing measures, columns and sentences are hidden rather than invented.

Account, data connections, permissions, and preferences belong in a secondary profile/settings area. Courses is a dedicated destination for the preparation of an athlete's goal races. `/races` follows the board's `V2 · Courses · Saison` artboards: on mobile, an upcoming, past and all filter kept in the URL (desktop always shows upcoming and past races); the featured race (highest priority, then nearest date) with its priority mark, kind (« Trail · ultra » from 60 km), countdown, date and start, the preparation reading as a coach line, distance, D+, target and average target pace, « Où tu en es → ce que la course demande » (#494: the longest run of the last six months against the plan's longest-run target for the distance, the best weekly D+ against the race D+ for trail races, and the best weekly running time against the minimum peak week of the matching plan template, each with a bar; a row without a demand is hidden), « Voir le plan de course » and « Modifier l’objectif »; then « À venir » as table rows (priority, date, race, kind, distance, D+, target, countdown and « Rôle dans la saison »: « Objectif de la saison » for an A race, « Répétition générale » for a B race before an A race, otherwise « Course importante », and « Test d’allure » or « Course de préparation » for a road or trail C race) and « Passées » with the matched activity's time linking to the activity and the outcome (« Objectif tenu », the delta over the target, or « Terminé » without a target) for a result on the same local date, a running activity and a distance within 15 %, or "Résultat non trouvé". Every figure comes from stored race data, the active plan, Garmin data or the computed course; there are no invented results, and trail and ultra races never show a predicted time. A context panel, beside the list from 1280px and after the races below it, holds « Saison », the season timeline with the active plan's blocks (races only without a plan) and the A, B and C legend, « Ce que tu vaux aujourd’hui » (the Garmin 10 km, half-marathon and marathon predictions with their change over four weeks, hidden without predictions), « Prochaine échéance » (the nearest dated race with its countdown and target pace), and suggests a B race when the featured race is an A race and none is planned. Races are added, edited and removed from Courses (#448), following the board's `V2 · Courses · Ajouter une course` artboards: « Ajouter une course » in the page header and the B-race suggestion (`?priority=B`) open `/races/new`, and « Modifier l’objectif » on the featured race and in the race workspace header opens `/races/<id>/edit`. Both are a three-step page (La course, Ton objectif, Effet sur le plan) with name, date, start, distance, D+, cutoff and road or trail, then the priority and an optional target; editing also offers « Supprimer la course » with a confirmation. The last step confirms the saved race. When a race is added while a plan is active (#510, workflow D of the coaching engine), it opens a plan-mode coach topic on the race with the Sports Director consulted and a request to propose the changes to review; the step follows that topic (« Le coach prépare ton plan… », a question to answer, no change, a failure or a decision) and, once the proposal is ready, shows its title and summary with « Examiner l’effet sur le plan », which opens the existing review at `/plan?proposal=<id>` with the before/after and the validator results. Nothing is applied before the athlete accepts, the race stays saved whatever the decision, and the athlete may leave the page while the coach works. Editing a race, or adding one without a plan, keeps the static confirmation with « Demander au coach ». Since #511 the first step also asks for the location and, for trail races, whether poles are allowed, forbidden or unknown; the second asks what the athlete expects from the race (Performance, Répétition générale, Plaisir), shows Stride's estimate next to the target when a past race with a matched result is comparable (Riegel scaling on the km-effort of the closest race, ±5 %, rounded to five minutes) and lets the athlete pick up to eight things to test from suggestions or their own. The location shows next to the race name, the expectation and things to test in Préparation, and the poles rule in Équipement. Prefilling from the Garmin calendar (#512) is not built yet. `/settings/goals` redirects to `/races`, and the season focus (« Quel est ton objectif ? ») lives in Réglages › Entraînement et staff under `#objectif`. Race workspaces live under `/races/<id>`. Athletes choose road or trail when creating a race and can change it later. Race workspaces carry the breadcrumb and the section tabs in the page header (scrolling pills on mobile) above a Dense race header. Since #495 the tabs follow `V2 · Courses · Proposition : la course le long d’un seul axe`. Trail races have four tabs: Plan de course (`/races/<id>`), with the views Plan, Côtes (`/races/<id>/climbs`, #498), Météo (`/races/<id>/weather`, #499) and Scénarios (`/races/<id>/scenarios`, the former Stratégie; `/roadbook` redirects there) in a segmented control at the end of the header (a second row below 1024px); Assistance (`/races/<id>/crew`, #501, `V2 · Courses · Plan de course · Assistance`) tells the crew where and when to meet the athlete: a coach line with how many aid stations are open to the crew, « Où et quand te retrouver » with each station whose services include « Assistance » and the finish (kilometre, planned time on the target, the window from the ambitious to the prudent scenario, or from the one that exists to the target, how to get there and what to prepare, both edited with « Modifier » and stored in `race_waypoints.crew_access` and `crew_prepare`), the map, « Sans assistance » with every other station and its reason (`crew_reason`, « Assistance non prévue » by default) and a link to open one from the waypoints, and « Imprimer la feuille d’assistance », which prints `/races/<id>/print?sheet=crew` with no health data. « Partager avec ton assistance » turns on a secret tracking link (`/crew/<token>`, a 32-character random token in `race_crew_links`, owner-only through RLS): no account is needed, the link only answers from the day before to the day after the race (in the course time zone) while it is switched on, « Créer un nouveau lien » replaces the token so the old one stops working, and « Ce que voit ton assistance » lists what is visible (the points and their windows, the access and what to prepare) and never shared (heart rate, health, feelings, live position). The public page reads the race through the `crew_link_view` security-definer function, which returns only the race name, date and kind, the plan timings, the course geometry, each waypoint's kind, name, kilometre, cutoff, services, stop and crew notes, and the strategist's pace factors, and nothing outside the active period; the page is not indexed and sends no referrer. An inactive link says so and asks for a new one. Once the race date has passed, an Après-course tab (`/races/<id>/after`, #430, `V2 · Courses · Après-course, sur ton plan`) reads the matched activity: a coach line with the time against the target and the section where the gap with the plan grew most, the time, average pace, average heart rate, D+ and distance, « Ta course sur ton plan » comparing the planned and actual race time at each station from the activity's distance and time series (only the finish without a detailed track), « Ta récupération » listing resting heart rate and HRV for up to ten nights after the race against their median over the 28 days before (« pas un avis médical »), « Ton ressenti » from the effort and feeling noted on the activity, and « Voir l’activité ». Without a matched activity it says « Résultat non trouvé » and how a result is matched; an upcoming race redirects to its plan. « Cette course » and « Comparer » switch the after-race views (#508): `/races/<id>/after/compare` finds the past editions of the same race (same name once accents and years are removed, same kind, distance within 15 %), lets the athlete pick the year when there are several, and compares time, average pace, average heart rate and distance, then section by section at the race's stations (or five equal sections) from both activities' distance and time series, with a coach line on the gain or loss and the section where it played most. « Refaire cette course » opens `/races/new?from=<id>` prefilled with the race's name without its year, kind, distance, D+, start, cutoff and priority, and the next edition's date estimated on the same weekday a year later, which the form asks to check. Scénarios (#500) opens on a coach line comparing the target with the prudent and ambitious scenarios, one card per scenario (target time and finish clock, average pace, walking threshold and distance, tightest cutoff margin including the race cutoff, « Hors de portée » when the simulation cannot reach it), « Voir le détail » to show its timeline below, « En faire mon objectif » which sets the race target to that scenario and frees its slot, with « Annuler » for 10 seconds, « Créer un scénario » for a missing prudent (slower than the target) or ambitious (faster) scenario, and « Heures de passage » comparing every station's passage time across scenarios. The strategist, the timeline and the printable roadbook follow below. Météo reads the weather where and when the athlete passes: a coach line with the rain window (kilometres and hours) and the finish temperature, the temperature range, rain or « Sec », the distance under rain, the strongest wind, when the night starts and the finish temperature, the temperature along the course with rain and night bands, « Ce que la météo change à ton plan » (a rain jacket before the first shower, warm layers under 8 °C, the head torch at the last station before night, drinking above 22 °C, a windproof layer from 30 km/h, each with its kilometre or time), the source, and « Si tu es en avance ou en retard » with each scenario's finish and distance under rain when it rains and several scenarios exist. Before the forecast window the page says it shows season averages and when the forecast starts. The header actions show as icons below 1536px. Côtes reads the climbs to walk: a coach line with the distance walked and the running pace that keeps the target, the profile with the walked climbs, the walking threshold as 6, 8, 10, 12 and 15 % (plus the current value) saved at once with « Annuler » for 10 seconds, the climbs to walk, the distance and D+ walked and the running pace, the table of climbs (kilometre, section, length, D+, average and maximum grade, passage time), and « Ce que change le seuil » comparing climbs, distance walked and running pace for each threshold. The target stays fixed, so the comparison shows the running pace rather than a finish delta; the walking card leaves the Plan view of a planned trail race. For a trail race with a simulated plan, the Plan view reads the race along one axis (#496): the map, « Au fil de la course » with a waypoint header (name, passage time and « barrière HH:MM »), then the Profil (elevation scale, walked climbs over the walking threshold), Allure (run pace per section, hidden below 640px), Météo (temperature at the passage time, « Météo · moyennes de saison » before the forecast) and Ravitaillement (g/h per section) lanes, each named above it at full width; rain is hatched and night darkened across every lane, labelled « Averses 14h–17h » and « Nuit dès 19:05 », and labels that would overlap are dropped, keeping the start, the finish and the cutoffs first. Kilometre ticks and a brand-blue « km · heure » pill sit under the lanes, « Sous le curseur » (kilometre and passage time, terrain, run or walk instruction with its pace, weather, what to take on the section, day or night and the next change within three hours) and the « Carnet de route » table by section (distance, D+, pace, walked distance, weather, what to take, passage and cutoff, with a total row). One cursor drives the lanes, a marker on the map, the panel and the current roadbook row; it moves with the pointer, the arrow keys (500 m, 1 km with Shift), Page Up/Down (5 km), Home and End, and a roadbook row moves it to its section. A click pins the cursor and a second click releases it; on touch it stays where the finger left it. Ctrl or ⌘ with the wheel, a pinch, or + and − zoom around the cursor down to a 5 km window; double-click or « Parcours entier » returns to the whole course. Beyond 60 km or once zoomed, « Aperçu du parcours » shows the whole profile with the window, which can be dragged or resized by its edges. Legend entries hide or show their band (walked climbs, rain, night, cutoffs), hovering or focusing a roadbook row highlights its section in the lanes, a waypoint name opens it in the waypoint editor (`?point=`), and the lanes fade while a setting recalculates, back to full after 8 seconds with a message if the recalculation has not returned. The plain wheel keeps scrolling the page. There is no heart-rate lane or ceiling until a source exists, and fueling shows carbs per section rather than individual intakes. Beside « Sous le curseur », « Régler la simulation » (#497) steps the target (5 min), the walking threshold (1 %, 5 to 20 %) and the stop per aid station (1 min) and shows the planned finish (with its day after midnight), the distance walked and its share of the course, the distance at night and where it starts, and the tightest cutoff margin. Each step saves through the existing race and race plan saves, so passage times, weather met, fueling and margins are recalculated together, and a toast offers « Annuler » for 10 seconds. Below it, « Emporter le plan » (#504, `V2 · Courses · Plan de course · Carnet de route à imprimer` and `Envoyer sur la montre`) opens « Envoyer sur la montre », which lists what the .fit file holds (course length, waypoints and instructions with their alerts), downloads it or the enriched GPX and says so, or shows an error when the file cannot be prepared; direct sending waits for the official Garmin Courses and Training APIs (#350, #351). « Carnet de route » previews `/races/<id>/print`, a paper-only page rendered from the plan at print time (light whatever the theme), in A4, folded A6 (fewer columns) or a wrist band (stations, kilometre, passage and cutoff), with the fueling, weather and walking columns optional; « Imprimer » prints the preview and « Ouvrir dans un onglet » opens it with the print dialog. Without a course only the roadbook is offered, its passages marked « ≈ ». Every change, undo and restore from the panel or the Côtes threshold is recorded in `race_setting_changes` (#519; owner-only insert and read, no update, removed with the race), and « Historique des réglages » under the panel lists the last ten changes, newest first, with when, the setting and its old and new values, and « Rétablir » to bring back the previous value through the same save. Recording is best effort: a failed record never undoes a saved setting. When a cutoff margin of the target scenario falls under 30 minutes (#503, `V2 · Courses · Plan de course · Barrière horaire menacée`), the Plan view opens on « Barrière horaire de … » (or « à l’arrivée » for the race cutoff) with the margin or the delay, a coach line with the passage and cutoff times, and up to three remedies, each with its resulting margin and « Appliquer »: shorter aid-station stops and walking from a steeper grade keep the running pace, so the target moves earlier by the time saved, and a faster target makes the whole plan earlier. Each remedy is recalculated by the simulation, only shows when it gains at least a minute, and stops at the first value giving 30 minutes back (otherwise the best one). Applying goes through the same setting saves and history as « Régler la simulation », with « Annuler » for 10 seconds; nothing changes until the athlete chooses, and « en parler au staff » opens the coach on the race. The context panel adds « Tes barrières » (every cutoff with its margin) and « Ce qui pèse sur la marge » (the stops before the cutoff, the walked climbs and the strategist's pace factors, each as the time it costs at that cutoff at the same running pace). The simulation has no weather or wind effect yet, so the board's rain and wind causes are not shown, and the day-of check-in note waits for #509. Otherwise the Plan view holds the former Parcours content (the on-track block, the map with French labels, Passages, Nuit, Marche and Averses layer chips and a fullscreen view over the workspace listing the segments, the profile with passages and weather, and a walking summary whose settings open with "Régler la marche"), the Scénarios view (scenario chips, the strategist block with the accepted instructions and any waiting proposal, the timeline alternating stations and segments with passage time, cutoff margin, weather, night, refills, pace, walk, carbs and the strategist's notes, a side summary with the arrival breakdown and waypoint status, and a printable roadbook), Ravitaillement (`/nutrition`, the former Alimentation, rebuilt in #505 on `V2 · Courses · Ravitaillement`: « Heure par heure », the carbs taken in each hour of the race spread from each section's moving time, against the target per hour, with caffeine and night marked, and « Section par section », what to carry in the pockets and what to do « Au ravito de … » with the planned stop, instead of the former table; since #528 « Testé à l’entraînement » records the carbs per hour tried on a long run (a running activity of 75 min or more in the last 180 days, or another outing with its date and duration), with Validé, Validé avec gêne or Pas toléré and an optional note, lists the tests and can delete them; the highest intake not marked Pas toléré caps the carbs used for the hourly and section allocation, the timeline, the gear plan and the nutritionist's reading, the card says whether the target is capped or covered, the editable target stays as entered, and the nutritionist cannot propose a target above the validated intake (`fueling_tests`, owner-only read, insert and delete); then the nutritionist banner, a flat row of five editable targets, the shared training base, the plan per segment, the start setup with products tested in training and the upcoming long sessions to test them, and the athlete's products from the brand catalogue or manual entry; the shopping list and a reminder that these are training references, not medical advice, sit in a context panel from 1280px and after the plan below), and Équipement (#506 adds « Ce que tu portes, au fil de la course » above the lists: the rain jacket over the rain window, gloves and a warm layer under 8 °C, a cap and sun cream from 22 °C and the head torch over the night, each drawn as kilometre ranges with where it starts or ends, following the forecast or the season averages before it, and the race pair's projected distance on race day from its last eight weeks of use; the readiness total across gear and preparation lists with what is left to buy and test, in a sticky panel from 1280px and first below, the race pair with its wear and break-in advice, Matériel obligatoire, Nuit, Selon la météo, Interdit, the Sac d’allègement at the first base de vie with the nutrition eaten after it and the athlete's own extras, then the J-7, J-1 and Jour J lists; each list shows its ready count (weather and forbidden items excluded, like the total), each row has an editable Prêt, À vérifier, À acheter or À tester status, unchecked preparation items show À faire, and the page prints as a check-list), and Préparation (`/preparation`, rebuilt in #507 on `V2 · Courses · Préparation`: the preparation reading as a coach line, « Le programme » strip of the race's season with its blocks, the current week and the races on it, « Où tu en es → ce que la course demande » shared with the season page, « Séances clés pour cette course » from the plan's key sessions in that season before the race with done, partial, missed or upcoming and the next six shown first, « Avant la course » from the race's preparation rows, and « Courses de préparation » between today and the race; an empty state once the race is past or undated, and a note when the race is not in the plan). Strategist and nutritionist proposals open in a review sheet (`?proposal=<id>`) on Scénarios or Ravitaillement, where the athlete accepts or refuses them. Road races have three tabs and no views: Plan de course, Équipement (the readiness total and the J-7, J-1 and Jour J lists, without gear lists) and Préparation. A past race's header reads « Terminé » and its date. The race header shows the countdown with the current plan phase and week, the distance, D+, start, target and average pace (plus the cutoff margin on mobile), the waypoint status and "Envoyer à la montre" for trail races, and "Poser une question", which opens `/coach/new?race=<id>` with the race attached. Trail races also have a waypoint editor (#502, `V2 · Courses · Plan de course · Modifier un point de passage`): the map, where the selected point is dragged along the course and snaps to it, then « Points de passage » as a table from 1024px (point, kilometre, type, services, cutoff, planned stop and passage time; a list below) with how many came from the GPX or were added and « + Ajouter », and the editor beside it (in a bottom sheet on mobile) with the planned passage, type, name, kilometre, cutoff, a planned stop of its own for an aid station or water point (empty keeps the common stop from « Régler la simulation »), services, watch settings and notes, with « Enregistrer », « Annuler » and « Supprimer ». A point's own stop is stored in `race_waypoints.stop_seconds` and used by the simulation, the timeline and the cutoff remedies. A course import ("Importer un GPX") is available from the desktop page header for both; A trail race without a course but with an announced distance and a target (#502, `V2 · Courses · Plan de course · Sans tracé`) gets an estimated plan instead of the import page: a coach line saying the passage times are an estimate from the announced distance and D+ with the estimated finish, a drop zone for the GPX (« Choisir un fichier », « Autres façons de l’ajouter »), « Au fil de la course, sans tracé » with the pace and fueling lanes, and the roadbook with the announced D+ spread evenly, no walking or weather, and every passage marked « ≈ ». The context panel lists what needs a course (« Avec le tracé ») and what works without one (« Disponible »), and « Pas de GPX officiel ? » links to the waypoint page, where aid stations are entered by hand with their announced kilometre (no map, profile, roadbook PDF or watch export without a course). Sections follow those stations; night, weather, climbs and the nutrition tab still need a course. replacing an existing course (#502, `V2 · Courses · Plan de course · Remplacer le tracé`) never happens on upload: a GPX file, a link or a Garmin activity first opens « Remplacer le tracé » with the file name, the distance, D+, waypoint count (and how many moved), walked climbs at the current threshold and every moved point with its old and new kilometre, the GPX points removed or new, and « Ce qui est gardé »; « Annuler » leaves the course untouched and « Remplacer » applies it. On replace, the athlete's own points are re-projected to the nearest place on the new course (or keep their kilometre when it no longer passes there) and marked « À vérifier », and GPX points matched by name keep their cutoff, services, watch settings and notes; start time and target are edited from their header values. The sidebar lists the athlete's races under Objectifs. Course-derived measures, simulations and weather carry their source or an explicit empty state when unavailable. The course D+ and D− sum the GPX elevations point by point with a 1 m hysteresis and no smoothing, so they match the organiser's figure; the smoothed profile is only drawn. A course imported from a Garmin activity takes the activity's recorded D+, and section D+ is scaled to add up to the course total (#483).

`/today` (Aujourd’hui) is the first destination and where onboarded athletes land. It follows the board's `V2 · Aujourd’hui` artboards: the decision of the day (session, structure bar, verdict and actions), « Ton corps ce matin » as a banded block with each signal in its habitual range, and the week as session rows, with « À faire » and the goal in a 308px context panel from 1280px; on mobile the same blocks stack with « À faire » after the decision. Today's session from the active plan shows its key targets and structure with a verdict that weighs it against the day's form: Séance maintenue, Avec prudence (with a coach link for a lighter version before a quality session), À revoir (with the reason and a coach review link), Forme inconnue without a Garmin readiness (with a sync action), or Récupère on a rest day. A quality session goes to review below a readiness of 50 or on an alert: an active pain report in Santé, VFC below its range two nights in a row, resting heart rate at least 7 bpm above its median, or a load ratio above 1,5; Récupération advice uses the same score thresholds. The verdict is hidden once the session is done. À faire shows one pending question at a time, in order the latest activity's feeling, then its pair, then the legs check-in shared with Récupération, with "x sur y" and "Plus tard"; answered questions leave the queue. « Ton corps ce matin » counts the signals outside their range and shows the Garmin readiness score and its 14-day gap, then VFC, resting heart rate, sleep and load, each with its personal band, value, threshold mark when one exists and a status, and whether the form is compatible with today's session. The current week lists each day with its state, intensity, duration, distance, D+ and completion, and opens the session panel; the goal race (countdown, date, distance, D+, current block and next lighter week) sits in the context panel. Rest days name the next session; without an active plan the session block links to Courses to add a race and to the coach. The adaptation banner, best moment and moving a session wait for their capabilities. Legs answers use the Récupération scale (Fraîches, Normales, Lourdes).

Plan shows the athlete's active stored plan. Its page header holds « Plan » and the tabs Programme (`/plan`), Calendrier (`/plan/calendar`) and Historique (`/plan/history`), as scrolling pills below 768px. `/plan` is the Programme complet of one season. The plan stays one continuous active plan ([ADR 0008](../adr/0008-versioned-plans-and-proposal-lifecycle.md)); seasons are derived from it for reading, one per A race, from the end of the previous season to that race's week when another A race follows, so the recovery opens the next season and the next objective becomes current once the race week is over (#468). The last A race's season ends with its recovery (the recovery window of its km-effort band, or the race week when the distance is unknown). Weeks after that recovery form a season « Hors objectif »; without an A race the primary race defines the season, and without a primary race the whole plan is « Hors objectif ». Seasons follow plan weeks, and week numbers restart at 1 in each season. B and C races have no programme of their own: they show as a marker on their week and in « Courses de la saison ». The season is kept in `?season=<race id>` (`open` outside an objective) and defaults to the current season; an unknown value shows the current season, a `?week=` or `?session=` without a season opens the season that contains it, and former `?race=<id>` links open the season that contains that race. A past season is read-only: it shows « Saison terminée · lecture seule », what was run (race day, sessions done, volume run, peak week), « Saison suivante » and « Revenir à la saison en cours ». A plan replaced by a `new_plan` proposal for the same A race joins that race's season (#486): its weeks up to the archive date and after the previous A race's week come before the newer plan's weeks, the newer plan wins a week both hold, and week numbers, the chart and the season summary cover the whole preparation. Those weeks are read-only: their session links carry `?plan=<plan id>` and open the archived plan's session panel, and a former `?season=plan-<plan id>` link opens the season that took the weeks. Every other replaced plan is an archived season at `?season=plan-<plan id>`, never the default and never reached from `?race=`, `?week=` or `?session=` alone. It is cut at its archive date (weeks starting after it are hidden), named after its primary race or else the plan title, and reads « Terminée » when that race is on or before the archive date, otherwise « Remplacé le <jour mois> » with « Plan remplacé · lecture seule » and no countdown. It shows its own sessions and stored matches; its session panel shows the planned session and the matched activity without check-in, shoe choice, watch, feeling, coach link or proposal; the page copies that plan and has no Historique. « Saison suivante » of the newest archived season leads to the active plan's first season. On mobile the season name next to the Plan title opens the same list as a bottom sheet. The page shows the weekly volume chart with a selectable week (kept in `?week=`, ← → on desktop), the selected week's days and progress, and milestones derived from the plan. Plan duration, distance and D+ count running sessions only. Distances without a declared value are estimated from the athlete's current pace per intensity and shown as approximate (≈); a week whose running distance cannot be estimated shows its target as approximate or no distance, never a partial sum. The sidebar lists blocks and weeks while Plan is open. There is no separate week list or session page. Calendrier (#427, board `V2 · Plan · Calendrier`) shows the whole plan as a Monday-first month kept in `?month=YYYY-MM`, defaulting to the current month and clamped to the plan's months, with ‹ / Aujourd’hui / › navigation. It holds the same weeks as Programme (#490): a replaced plan's weeks that joined an active season come first, read-only, so the first month is the start of the preparation; their sessions keep their done, partial or missed state, count in the month's summary, and open the archived plan's session panel through `?plan=<plan id>`, which only opens a panel for a plan whose weeks joined an active season. Archived seasons of their own are not shown. Each day lists its sessions with their intensity, duration and « fait », rest days and today; arrow keys, Home and End move between days and Enter opens the day's first session in the same panel, kept in `?session=`. From 1280px a context panel gives the month's planned and done running time and distance, session and quality counts, light weeks, and the races to come; on mobile a compact grid with intensity dots selects a day and lists its week below. Opening a session shows it in a side panel on desktop and a bottom sheet on mobile, with previous and next sessions of the week, ← → and Échap; the session is kept in `?session=<key>` next to `?season=` and `?week=`, so loading that URL directly opens Plan with the same panel, and an unknown key shows Plan without a panel. Outside Plan, a session is a panel and Plan is navigation: every other screen that links to a session (Today, Récupération, Coach attachments, the activity debrief, Matériel) links to `/plan/sessions/<key>`, which opens the same panel over the current page without loading Plan. Previous and next replace the URL and stay over that page; Échap, the close button, the overlay or browser Back return to it with its scroll and focus on the link that opened the panel, and a link inside the panel leaves it for its target. Loading `/plan/sessions/<key>` directly redirects to `/plan?session=<key>`. Coach proposals still open in Plan. A race day opens its race page instead of the panel, and race distance and D+ come from the imported course when one exists, otherwise from the values typed for the race. A completed session compares planned and completed values from the matched Garmin activity. Accepting proposals, moving or replacing sessions, sending to the watch, changing the matched activity and coach comments wait for their capabilities.

Santé is served from `/health` and sits between Activités and Courses. Its page header holds « Santé » and the tabs Forme, Récupération, Records and Nutrition (scrolling pills below 768px), and the sidebar lists the same sections while it is open; Vue d’ensemble and Journée wait for #429, so `/health` redirects to Forme. Each tab follows the board's `V2 · Santé` artboards: a reading (kicker, verdict title, summary and the scale or period selector), banded widgets in the main column and a 308px context panel from 1280px. `/health/fitness` is Forme: the goal race widget with its plan phase, a period selector kept in the URL, the Garmin condition and fatigue chart with an actionable note, running km against the plan, intensity distribution and Garmin race predictions, with the measures that evolve in the context panel. `/health/recovery` is Récupération, with Jour, 14 jours and 30 jours scales: readiness with advice for today's session, what weighs today, nights, HRV and resting HR, daily indicators, the daily check-in and HRV recovery after past races. The former `/progress` URLs redirect permanently to their tab (`/progress` to Forme, `/progress/body`, `/progress/recovery` and `/progress/health` to Récupération, `/progress/records` to Records) and keep their query. Actions open the plan, the source activities or a prefilled Coach request. Definitions are in [Progress measures](../architecture/progress.md).

`/health/records` is Records: a deterministic reading, the record within reach of its Garmin prediction, current markers (threshold pace and heart rate, VO₂ max, VAM), road speed records with Garmin predictions and endurance records, each linked to its source activity.

Coach is one group conversation with a staff of five roles: Coach, Santé, Directeur sportif, Stratège de course and Nutrition. Following the Stride Design System page « Le staff », a role has no first name, no human face and no colour: it is a galet, one constant silhouette per role at 48, 30 or 22px with its role name always written next to it. A galet idles, thinks while its role works and speaks with the only `primary` halo while its role writes; with reduced motion it stays still. The same role names appear in proposals, notifications, nutrition and race screens, and the coaching agents refer to each other by role.

Coach has one main thread and topics (#463, board `V2 · Coach · Fil principal`, `Liste des sujets`, `Un sujet` and `Nouveau sujet`). `/coach` is the main thread, for day-to-day exchanges: one continuous timeline, even though the staff starts a fresh session each day and after a cost limit, and the athlete never sees it end. Its desktop header holds the « Conversation » and « Le staff » tabs, « Sujets N » (the open topics) and « Nouveau sujet »; on mobile a « Fil principal · N sujets ▾ » bar opens the same list as a bottom sheet, which also leads to the staff page. The list shows the main thread, « Sujets ouverts » and « Sujets clos » with the leading role, message count, decisions and date. « Nouveau sujet » asks for a name, offers four themes and an optional race link, then opens an empty topic; its first message creates it. A topic at `/coach/<id>` has the breadcrumb « Coach › titre », « Renommer », « Clore le sujet » and a « Ce sujet » panel (« Lié à », « Mené par », the first specialist who spoke or else Coach, « Avec », « Décisions », and « Revenir au fil principal »). Its messages stay out of the main thread. Closing it makes it read-only and adds a card to the main thread: « Sujet clos · titre », its dates, a short summary, its decisions and message count, and « Relire le sujet ». A topic that reaches its cost limit can only be closed. Conversations from before #463 are closed topics without a card. `/coach/new?…` links redirect to `/coach` with the same prefill, and a notification for a main-thread session redirects to `/coach`.

Global search (#413, board `V2 · Système · Composants : la recherche (⌘K)`) opens from the centred field of the desktop top bar (from 1024px), the magnifier of the mobile top bar, ⌘K or Ctrl+K anywhere, and / outside a field or dialog. It is a dialog on desktop and full screen below 768px. On opening it shows « Récents » (the three latest of the last five opened results and last three queries, kept in the browser and cleared with « Effacer l’historique de recherche »), « Aller à » and « Actions ». From the second character it lists, in this order, « Actions » when the query starts with an action verb, « Séances » (upcoming first, then the most recent), « Activités » (most recent first, the latest 500) and « Courses ». `GET /api/search` returns the athlete's own entries under row-level security with French labels when the dialog opens; ranking runs in the browser on each keystroke and ignores case and accents, prefers word starts over substrings over one-letter typos, and highlights the matched text. The leading group shows five results and the others three; « Voir les N autres » opens the filtered activity list or expands the group. A session opens its panel, an activity or a race its page. ↑ ↓ move, Entrée opens, ⌘/Ctrl+Entrée opens in a new tab, Tab moves to the next group, and Échap clears the query, then closes. Without a result it offers to ask the coach the query. Conversation, health, settings, a context chip and date or number criteria from the board are not searched yet.

« Rechercher » (⌘/Ctrl+F on the main thread and topic pages only, a search button in the mobile bar) opens a search bar over the conversation (#464, board `V2 · Coach · Recherche dans la conversation`). It searches, through the server and under row-level security, the athlete's own messages and answers, the staff's messages and questions, proposal titles and summaries, and the text and decisions of topic-summary cards, in the main thread and every titled topic, closed ones included, within the most recent 5 000 items. Matching ignores case and accents and takes wildcards and punctuation literally; a query needs two characters. The query lives in `?q=`, the current result in `&at=<item id>`, a narrowed « Chercher dans » filter in `&scope=` and a date jump in `&date=`. Results are one chronological sequence: opening a search lands on the most recent, « N sur M » is announced politely, ↑ (or Entrée) goes to the older result and ↓ (or Maj+Entrée) to the newer one, a result in another conversation opens it with the same search, and a result outside the loaded messages loads that conversation around it. Matches are highlighted and the current one is filled and scrolled into view. From 1280px the right panel lists the results, newest first, with date, author and excerpt; matches in a closed topic form one « Sujet clos · titre » row with its message and decision counts; four rows show before « Voir les N autres »; « Chercher dans » offers Fil principal, Sujets and Décisions (proposals and topic-summary decisions). « Aller à une date » scrolls to the first message on or after that day. On mobile only the bar shows, with the counter, ↑, ↓ and « Fermer ». Échap or « Fermer » drops the search from the URL and returns focus to « Rechercher ».

The race profile keeps passage labels short so nearby waypoints remain readable. Hovering or focusing a waypoint badge reveals its full name and passage details in a row below the chart. Inspecting the elevation line uses the same row, leaving the plotted data visible.

The secondary `/settings` area links from the account menu and follows the board's `V2 · Réglages` artboards: a settings sidebar headed « Retour à Stride » with the groups « Toi » (Vue d’ensemble, Profil et zones, Entraînement et staff, Notifications, Apparence et raccourcis) and « Données » (Connexions with the Garmin status, Données et confidentialité), a « Réglages › page » header and a centred 720px column. Profile and training edits reuse the onboarding records and preserve Garmin provenance on unchanged values. Goal and race edits use the same persisted records and revision checks. Connection pages show observed integration status and stored counts, and the Garmin page offers "Synchroniser maintenant". They do not offer reconnect or disconnect until the Garmin service exposes those operations. Data/privacy provides an authenticated JSON copy of profile, goal and race records. `/settings/notifications` holds the push notification settings for the current device and the four push categories, and `/settings/appearance` the theme choice (Système, Clair, Sombre) and the keyboard shortcuts. The overview counts the athlete-model facts from Garmin, the athlete and Stride estimates, and lists the open questions (missing maximum or threshold heart rate, goal focus, required profile and training answers) with what each one improves and a link to the field that answers it; the first three show and the rest fold behind "Voir les N autres". The profile shows the heart rate zones (Karvonen or share of maximum heart rate, with their inputs) and the pace zones per intensity with the threshold pace source, date and confidence, or an empty state naming what is missing. Settings show no control without a working action: units and coach preferences are absent until they exist, and account or Garmin data deletion is requested by e-mail from the privacy page. Garmin activity export awaits its underlying capability.

Notifications are not a sidebar destination. The desktop top bar and every mobile header carry a bell; its accessible name gives the unread count and both show a dot, never a count. On desktop it opens a panel with the 20 latest notifications of the last 30 days, the Toutes, À traiter, Staff and Données filters, "Tout marquer comme lu" and "Voir tout l’historique". On mobile the bell links to `/notifications`, which groups the last 30 days by day in Europe/Paris with the same filters kept in `?filter=`. Activating a notification marks it read and opens its existing destination: `/plan?proposal=<id>` for a proposal, `/coach/<thread>` for a staff reply or question, `/activities/<id>` for a Garmin activity or a shoe question, `/settings/connections/garmin` for a connection problem and `/plan` for a watch send. Opening a Coach thread marks its notifications read. The `/notifications` history keeps its filters and « Tout marquer comme lu » in a toolbar, and from 1280px a « 30 derniers jours » panel gives the unread and to-do counts.

Garmin sources are listed under Données and detected on open rather than pushed: after the application shell renders, Stride runs `reconcile_garmin_notifications` in the background under the athlete session. It reads the Garmin service tables of the last 30 days and creates, updates or resolves notifications, so a new Garmin event appears on the next screen the athlete opens. A running activity imported less than 48 hours after it started is announced as « Nouvelle activité », associated with a session of the active plan or unplanned; the historical import creates nothing. A connection that needs reconnecting is to review, a failing sync only informs, and both resolve as « Rétablie » once the connection is back. A watch send that failed or waits for confirmation is to review until it is sent. A shoe pair that differs from the planned one asks « Quelle paire pour … ? » until the athlete answers; the shoe check runs in Stride, so shoe questions are reconciled only from the pair checks the Today, gear and activity screens already compute. A notification that resolves on its own is also marked read. Past-dated watch sessions are no longer to review. The reconcile is idempotent, never makes a read notification unread unless the event itself recurs, and never blocks or breaks the page. For athletes with a push device, the minute push tick also runs the same reconcile in Postgres, so a run imported while the app is closed is announced by push ([ADR 0014](../adr/0014-postgres-scheduled-push-delivery.md)).

## Primary navigation paths

1. First session: Today explains the next step and guides the athlete to complete only the context needed to get value.
2. Training review: Today or Plan opens a weekly proposal; a decision links to the evidence and Coach for questions.
3. Activity review: Activities opens an individual run, then offers relevant trends or a coaching question.
4. Event preparation: Courses opens the next priority race and its course, passage plan, nutrition, equipment and preparation checklists.

## Information hierarchy

- Lead with the next decision, not a grid of all available metrics.
- Keep source, freshness, and uncertainty next to the data they qualify.
- Put detailed calculations and activity fields behind a clear drill-down.
- Keep Coach available from relevant contexts and pass only authorized context to it.
- Use the same activity concepts for road and trail while allowing terrain-specific detail where it changes interpretation.

## Open UX questions

- Does an athlete think first in terms of a weekly plan or a daily recommendation?
- Should Coach be a persistent destination, a contextual action, or both?
- Which metrics are understandable and useful to the initial athlete segment?

Validate these choices with the selected athlete journey before designing a full screen inventory.
